02 DOCTRINE WHY IT EXISTED

A fighter is a compromise.
The Tomcat refused to choose.

Swing wings gave it the turning circle of a lightweight and the dash speed of a missile. Two crews, two engines and six Phoenix rounds made it a twelve-tonne airframe that could track and kill six targets at once, from a hundred miles out.

It was overkill by design. That was the point. For thirty-six years the F-14 was the thing a carrier group put between itself and the horizon — fast enough to arrive first, heavy enough to stay.

05 SERVICE HISTORY 36 YEARS · 1970—2006

Thirty-six years
on the pointy end.

  1. 1967

    The Navy walks

    The F-111B was a bomber that could not dogfight. The Navy cancels it and writes VFX-1: a dedicated fleet-defence fighter built around the AWG-9 radar and the Phoenix missile.

    PROGRAM
  2. 1969

    Grumman wins

    The Model 303 — a twin-engine, two-seat fighter with a titanium wing box — is selected. Twelve development airframes are ordered.

    CONTRACT
  3. 1970

    First flight

    21 December. F-14A BuNo 157980 lifts off from Grumman's Calverton field with Bob Smyth and William Miller aboard. The swing wing works.

    MILESTONE
  4. 1972

    Fleet introduction

    VF-1 Wolfpack and VF-2 Bounty Hunters stand up at NAS Miramar. For a moment, the Tomcat is the most capable interceptor on earth.

    SQUADRON
  5. 1981

    First blood

    19 August, Gulf of Sidra. Two VF-41 Tomcats down two Libyan Su-22s with Sidewinders — the type's first air-to-air kills.

    COMBAT
  6. 1986

    More thrust

    The F-14A+ swaps the troublesome TF30 for the F110-GE-400, adding thirty percent more thrust and finally giving the airframe the engines it deserved. Redesignated F-14B in 1991.

    POWERPLANT
  7. 1991

    Desert Storm

    Tomcats fly escort, reconnaissance and strike control over Iraq. The air war is effectively over before the Phoenix is ever needed.

    COMBAT
  8. 1992

    Super Tomcat

    The F-14D reaches the fleet with the APG-71 radar, digital avionics and a glass cockpit. It is the definitive Tomcat — and the last.

    VARIANT
  9. 2006

    Sundown

    22 September. A VF-31 Tomcat lands at NAS Oceana, closing the type's career with the U.S. Navy. The last cats are struck from the record.

    RETIREMENT

Iran received 79 F-14A airframes before 1979; a small number remain the last flying Tomcats in the world.

07 ORDNANCE SIX AT ONCE

Six answers
to one question.

The AWG-9 could hold six targets at once. The Phoenix was built to service all six before they ever came into view. Everything else on the jet — the wings, the engines, the second seat — existed to make that possible.

TWO ENGINES. TWO CREWS. SIX PHOENIX.
